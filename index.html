Glossary

The Peruvian words on our menu, explained. In the menu, tap any underlined word to see what it means.

A lo macho
Topped with a spicy, creamy seafood sauce.
A lo pobre
Served with a fried egg and fried sweet plantain.
Aeropuerto
Chifa classic: chaufa tossed with stir-fried noodles and bean sprouts.
Aguadito
Thick cilantro rice soup.
Ají amarillo
Peru's golden chili pepper: fruity, with medium heat.
Ají limo
Small, very spicy and fragrant chili; the classic one in ceviche.
Ají panca
Dried red chili, smoky and mild; the base of the anticucho marinade.
Algarrobina
Syrup made from the carob trees of northern Peru.
Anticuchero
Marinated and grilled anticucho-style, with ají panca.
Anticucho
Beef-heart skewers marinated in ají panca and grilled; an Afro-Peruvian street classic.
Cancha
Toasted, salted Andean corn kernels; the crunchy side of ceviche.
Carretillero
Street-cart style: crowned with crispy fried calamari.
Cau cau
Creole stew of beef tripe and diced potatoes in ají amarillo, finished with mint.
Causa
Cold mashed yellow potato seasoned with lime and ají amarillo, layered with a filling.
Cecina
Salt-cured, smoked pork from the Peruvian Amazon.
Chalaca
Callao-style topping of diced onion, tomato, rocoto and corn with lime.
Chancho asado
Cantonese-style roast pork, sweet and lacquered.
Chaufa
Peruvian-Chinese fried rice, tossed in a very hot wok with egg, scallion and soy sauce.
Chifa
Peruvian-Chinese cuisine, born from Cantonese immigrants in Lima in the 19th century.
Chijaukay
Crispy fried chicken glazed in a dark oyster-and-soy sauce with scallions.
Chilcano
Pisco with ginger ale and lime.
Choclo
Andean corn with large, tender kernels.
Choros
Mussels.
Chorrillana
Sautéed onion, tomato and ají, in the style of Chorrillos, Lima.
Chupe
Creamy chowder with milk, fresh cheese, egg, potato and rice.
Corvina
Corvina (white sea bass): firm white fish, Peru's favorite for ceviche.
Cusqueña
Peruvian lager from Cusco.
Huancaína
Creamy sauce of fresh cheese and ají amarillo.
Huevera
Fish roe, breaded and fried.
Jalea
Platter of breaded, fried fish and seafood over yuca, with salsa criolla.
Juane
Amazonian rice with chicken, egg and olive, seasoned with palillo and steamed in bijao leaves.
Kam lu
Sweet-and-sour chifa sauce, served over crispy fried wontons.
Leche de tigre
The citrus, ají and fish juice that cures ceviche, served as a shot.
Limón kay
Crispy chicken in a sweet lemon sauce.
Maduros
Ripe sweet plantains, fried.
Mariquitas
Thin, crispy plantain chips.
Mensi
Fermented brown soybean paste, a Cantonese seasoning.
Nabo encurtido
Chinese-style pickled turnip.
Palta
Avocado.
Pargo
Red snapper.
Parihuela
Hearty seafood soup with ají panca and ají amarillo, from Peru's coast.
Picarones
Sweet potato and squash doughnuts with chancaca (raw cane sugar) syrup.
Pisco
Peru's grape brandy.
Rachi
Beef tripe marinated in ají panca like anticuchos and grilled; a Lima street-food classic.
Rocoto
Round, very spicy Andean red pepper.
Salsa criolla
Fresh relish of red onion, ají and lime.
Saltado
Stir-fried in a very hot wok with onion, tomato and soy: the chifa touch in Creole cooking.
Seco
Beef slowly braised in a cilantro sauce.
Sudado

The Konakai experience

Three decades of Peruvian cooking, one family.

Konakai has been a family restaurant since 1992. We cook the whole of Peru: lime-cured ceviches from the coast, Creole classics like lomo saltado and ají de gallina, and the Chifa, the Peruvian-Chinese wok cooking that made us famous.

Fresh fish, real Peruvian ají and recipes passed down at home. A warm, family room made for birthdays, Sunday lunches and long dinners with friends.

  • Chifa specialistsFried rice and wok-fired saltados with real smoky flavor.
  • From the seaCeviches, parihuela, chalaca mussels and octopus anticuchero.
  • Flavors of the AmazonTacacho, cecina and juane, hard to find anywhere else in Florida.
